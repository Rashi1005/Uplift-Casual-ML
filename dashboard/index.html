The honest headline

Every model's confidence interval crosses zero and overlaps every other model's. At this sample size, none of the four — including the naive baseline — can be shown to beat random targeting with statistical confidence.

The Two-Model Approach has the highest point estimate (0.0234), but that alone isn't proof it's the best strategy.

Consistent with Diemert et al. (2018), the paper behind the Criteo uplift benchmark, which found similar statistical indistinguishability at comparable sample sizes.

Who are we actually targeting?

A response model can only tell you who is likely to visit. It can't tell you whether that visit happens because of the email. Every customer actually falls into one of four groups — only one of them is worth spending budget on.

Select a group above to see why a standard response model can't tell it apart from the others.

What we actually measured

Our naive baseline model ranks customers by predicted visit probability. Its top decile visited at a real, verified rate of 24.06%, against 13.71% for everyone else — a genuine 10.4-point gap.

That gap mixes Sure Things and Persuadables together — a response model has no way to separate them. That's the exact gap the uplift models on this dashboard are built to close.
